GSA Bulletin · 2024

Reading deformation in rivers and terraces

Rivers and abandoned floodplains record uneven uplift across the southern Colorado Front Range and western High Plains.

Journal article

The question

Does renewed river incision reflect a more erosive climate, deformation of the land, or both?

How we approached it

We combined bedrock river-profile analysis, fluvial terraces, and flexural and geomorphic analyses in the upper Arkansas River region.

What we found

  • Knickpoints record increases in the rate of base-level fall.
  • Both river profiles and terraces show incision increasing eastward.
  • The combined evidence supports regional west-directed backtilting associated with differential rock uplift.

Why it matters

Matching evidence across active channels and preserved terraces helps separate landscape responses to climate from deformation of the land itself.

Citation

Marder, E.; Gallen, S. F.; Pazzaglia, F. J. (2024). Late Cenozoic deformation in the U.S. southern Colorado Front Range revealed by river profile analysis and fluvial terraces. GSA Bulletin 136(3–4), 1067–1085. https://doi.org/10.1130/B36440.1
