Geology · 2023

How climate changes the erosion–steepness link

A global comparison shows that climate changes how river steepness relates to erosion rate.

Journal articleOpen preprint

The question

Why can landscapes eroding at similar rates have different river gradients?

How we approached it

We compared cosmogenic beryllium-10 erosion rates and normalized channel steepness in near-steady-state, river-dominated basins underlain by crystalline rocks, then interpreted the patterns with river-incision theory.

What we found

  • The erosion–steepness relationship becomes more nonlinear in wetter climates.
  • More efficient erosion in humid settings allows lower fluvial relief at a given erosion rate.
  • Patterns are consistent with climate-related differences in discharge variability when incision thresholds matter.

Why it matters

Climate must be considered when using river topography to interpret erosion and mountain-building processes.

Citation

Marder, E.; Gallen, S. F. (2023). Climate control on the relationship between erosion rate and fluvial topography. Geology 51(5), 424–427. https://doi.org/10.1130/G50832.1
