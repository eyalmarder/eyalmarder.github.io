Geomorphology · 2018

Active tectonics in the Lower Jordan basin

River geometry and geophysical evidence reveal how active deformation shapes the Lower Jordan River basin.

Journal article

The question

Where does active tectonic deformation leave a recognizable signature in the river landscape?

How we approached it

We combined remote sensing, image processing, and measurements of river shape with mapped faults, gravity information, and earthquake records along the Dead Sea Transform.

What we found

  • Changes in channel sinuosity, slope, and width coincide with zones of deformation.
  • River and gravity patterns, supported by seismicity, indicate localized uplift and faulting.
  • The evidence places substantial tectonic activity near the basin center and main fault system.

Why it matters

Combining surface measurements with geophysical observations helps identify active deformation where direct access is difficult.

Citation

Marder, E.; Bookman, R.; Filin, S. (2018). Geomorphological response of the Lower Jordan River basin to active tectonics of the Dead Sea Transform. Geomorphology 317, 75–90. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.geomorph.2018.05.018
