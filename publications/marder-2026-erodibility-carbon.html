Science Advances · 2026

Rock strength, erosion, and carbon

In Colorado’s East River watershed, weak shale can erode rapidly beneath gentle terrain, with consequences for weathering and carbon.

Journal articleOpen access

The question

How does bedrock erodibility connect mountain topography, erosion, and weathering-related CO₂ release?

How we approached it

We combined cosmogenic beryllium-10 measurements, topographic analysis, river chemistry, and landscape evolution models across contrasting shale and intrusive rocks. The models examine fluvial adjustment and inorganic weathering during ice-free intervals.

What we found

  • Shale catchments erode faster despite their gentler slopes.
  • Models show that contrasting rock strengths can sustain long-lived differences in landscape adjustment.
  • Chemistry and modeling link enhanced weathering to net inorganic-weathering CO₂ release.

Why it matters

Rock properties help explain why steepness alone can misrepresent erosion and why changing landscapes affect carbon cycling.

Citation
