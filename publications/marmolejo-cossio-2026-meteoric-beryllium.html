Geochronology · 2026

An atmospheric tracer in mountain soils

Soils on dated glacial moraines reveal how atmospheric beryllium-10 reaches a mountain watershed.

Journal articleOpen access

The question

How can we calibrate atmospheric beryllium-10 delivery so that soil inventories become reliable records of surface processes?

How we approached it

We measured soil inventories on five dated moraines across an elevation gradient in the East River watershed, accounting for inherited beryllium-10 and evaluating potential losses.

What we found

  • Local delivery varies with elevation, precipitation, and snow conditions.
  • Local calibration resolves variation within the watershed. Global circulation model averages broadly agree with the estimates, while a precipitation-based empirical model overpredicts local fluxes.

Why it matters

A locally calibrated tracer can expand erosion and weathering research into quartz-poor terrain, where conventional in-situ beryllium-10 measurements are difficult.

Citation

Marmolejo-Cossío, J. M.; Larsen, I. J.; Marder, E.; Hidy, A. J. (2026). Meteoric beryllium-10 fluxes from soil inventory measurements in the East River watershed, Colorado, USA. Geochronology 8(3), 547–565. https://doi.org/10.5194/gchron-8-547-2026
