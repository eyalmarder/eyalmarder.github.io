GSA Today · 2025

Why the Rockies are cutting down again

River profiles, erosion measurements, and dated terraces reveal renewed landscape change long after the Colorado Rockies first formed.

Journal articleOpen accessArticle PDF

The question

What explains renewed incision and relief in an old mountain belt?

How we approached it

We examined river profiles along the eastern Colorado Rocky Mountain front and combined cosmogenic beryllium-10 erosion measurements with luminescence-dated river terraces.

What we found

  • Migrating knickpoints separate gentler, slower-eroding uplands from steeper, faster-eroding lower reaches.
  • River steepness below knickpoints increases from north to south.
  • The regional pattern is consistent with differential rock uplift and broad geodynamic forcing.

Why it matters

Old mountain belts can remain dynamic, with deep Earth processes influencing river incision long after their initial construction.

Citation

Marder, E.; Gallen, S. F.; Lifton, N. A.; Rittenour, T. M. (2025). The Colorado Rocky Mountains awaken: Understanding topographic rejuvenation in postorogenic mountain belts. GSA Today 35(1), 4–11. https://doi.org/10.1130/GSATG598A.1
