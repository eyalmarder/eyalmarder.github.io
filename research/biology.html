Research perspective

A mountain range changes more than elevation. As relief develops, it changes where organisms can move, the environments they encounter, and the opportunities for populations to become isolated. My work connects the physical evolution of landscapes with biological processes, asking how mountain building leaves a signature in biodiversity and how long that signature takes to develop.

Published in Science, 2025

Linking uplift, dispersal, and speciation

How do the pace and magnitude of mountain building influence biodiversity?

I co-developed coupled landscape and biological models of small-mammal dispersal, adaptation, and speciation. The simulations show how greater uplift supports more species in mountains and neighboring depositional basins.

Understanding delayed biological responses

Can biodiversity retain a record of earlier landscape change?
