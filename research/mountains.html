Research perspective

Mountains continue to change long after their initial formation. Rivers cut through bedrock, terraces preserve earlier valley floors, and climate affects how water drives erosion. I combine these records to investigate how tectonics, hydroclimate, and rock properties shape landscapes, from active fault systems to the Colorado Rocky Mountains and global river networks.

Published in GSA Bulletin and GSA Today

The Colorado Rocky Mountains awaken

What explains renewed relief in a mountain belt long after its main mountain-building phase?

In the southern Colorado Front Range and western High Plains, I used river profiles and dated terraces to identify late Cenozoic deformation. This work connects preserved landforms with the history of river incision and mountain rejuvenation. A subsequent synthesis considers how these observations inform the evolution of postorogenic mountain belts.

Published in Geology, 2023

Climate changes how steepness records erosion

Does the same river steepness imply the same erosion rate in different climates?

My global analysis shows that hydroclimate changes the relationship between erosion rate and river steepness. Interpreting channel form therefore requires attention to the hydrologic conditions under which erosion occurs. This provides a physical foundation for comparing landscapes across climate regimes.
