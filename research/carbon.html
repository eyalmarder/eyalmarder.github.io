Research perspective

Erosion exposes minerals, moves sediment, and changes the pathways through which water meets rock. Those physical changes influence chemical weathering and carbon exchange. My research in Colorado's East River watershed asks how differences in bedrock affect the pace of landscape adjustment and the chemical products delivered downstream.

Published in Science Advances, 2026

Why gentle terrain can erode quickly

How do contrasting rock types control erosion during landscape adjustment?

In East River, gentle shale terrain erodes faster than neighboring, steeper intrusive terrain. Erosion measurements, river chemistry, and landscape models link this contrast to differences in bedrock erodibility and prolonged landscape adjustment.

Connecting erosion to inorganic-weathering carbon pathways

What are the chemical consequences of prolonged landscape adjustment?

River chemistry and landscape models connect shale erosion to enhanced solute generation and net CO₂ release from inorganic weathering. The calculations track these reactions during ice-free intervals as the landscape adjusts.
