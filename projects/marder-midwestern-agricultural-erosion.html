Tracing soil redistribution in Midwestern croplands

Fallout cesium-137, terrain measurements, and soil-transport modeling investigate how soil moves through cultivated landscapes.

The question

How has soil redistribution varied during the conservation era?

Following soil with fallout tracers

Cesium-137 binds to soil particles and records their redistribution since the mid-twentieth century. I combine soil profiles from cultivated fields and native-prairie reference sites with LiDAR terrain measurements and a soil-transport model to investigate local surface lowering and its relationship with relief.

Fallout cesium-137Cultivated fields and native-prairie referencesLiDAR terrain analysisSoil transport modeling

Building on earlier field studies

Earlier work measured soil loss at prairie–cropland boundaries and used cosmogenic beryllium-10 to investigate erosion before cultivation. This project uses cesium-137 to examine soil redistribution over a more recent timescale.
