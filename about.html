About

A curiosity that crosses
scales and planets.

I study landscape evolution—and the connections it creates among the physical environment, soils, carbon, and life.

Rivers, hillslopes, soils, and drainage networks preserve records of landscape change. I combine field observations, geochemical tracers, remote sensing, and numerical models to connect their form with the rates and processes that shaped them.

My research spans soil redistribution in Midwestern croplands, mountain evolution in the Rockies, and drainage networks on Mars. Coupled models explore the links between mountain building and biodiversity, while erosion measurements and river chemistry reveal the carbon consequences of weathering.

I am a postdoctoral researcher in the Department of Earth, Geographic, and Climate Sciences at the University of Massachusetts Amherst, working with Isaac J. Larsen on lithologic controls on denudation, weathering, and alpine landscape evolution. Previously, I worked with Brian J. Yanites and Tara M. Smiley at Indiana University Bloomington on mountain building, climate, and biodiversity. I received my Ph.D. in Geosciences from Colorado State University, advised by Sean F. Gallen.
